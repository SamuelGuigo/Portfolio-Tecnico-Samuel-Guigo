# Fiber & Optical Link Troubleshooting

**Role:** Network Infrastructure Engineer  
**Type:** Troubleshooting case

## Objective

Troubleshoot unstable or degraded optical connectivity by combining transceiver information, optical measurements and physical-link analysis.

## My approach

I use evidence from both the active network equipment and the passive optical path.

The workflow can include:

- checking interface state and counters;
- validating SFP/SFP+ compatibility;
- reviewing transmit and receive optical power;
- checking wavelength requirements;
- validating connector and fiber type;
- reviewing WDM direction and pairing;
- inspecting patching and physical route;
- comparing expected and measured link behavior;
- using OTDR or field-test evidence when available.

## Diagnostic reasoning

An optical link can fail even when both interfaces appear administratively correct.

Potential causes include:

- excessive attenuation;
- dirty or damaged connectors;
- incorrect fiber type;
- incompatible optics;
- wrong WDM pairing;
- bend or splice loss;
- incorrect patching;
- failing transceiver;
- excessive distance for the optic budget.

I reduce the hypothesis space before recommending physical intervention.
